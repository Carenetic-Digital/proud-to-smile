Did you know that [50%](https://www.cdc.gov/oralhealth/conditions/periodontal-disease.html) of adults over 30 and almost three-quarters of seniors in the United States have periodontal (gum) disease? Gingivitis, the mildest form of gum disease, is the first step to advanced periodontal disease.

Gingivitis develops when your teeth have a buildup of [plaque](https://www.healthline.com/health/dental-and-oral-health/plaque), a sticky film that forms when bacteria in your mouth combine with the sugars in food. If not effectively removed with brushing and flossing, it hardens into [tartar](https://www.webmd.com/oral-health/guide/tartar-dental-calculus-overview#1), creating a layer where harmful bacteria thrive above — and below — the gum line.

[Gingivitis symptoms](https://www.webmd.com/oral-health/gingivitis-gum-11#1-3) are mild at first, but worsen as the disease progresses. They include:

- Bad breath
- Tenderness with flossing
- Gums that bleed after brushing or flossing
- Mild inflammation of the gums

## Why reversing gingivitis is important

Gingivitis is the first step toward more serious gum disease. Periodontal disease is an infection of the gums, and it affects your oral and overall health. It increases your risk of dental decay and tooth loss, and it’s [associated with serious health conditions](https://www.perio.org/for-patients/gum-disease-information/gum-disease-and-other-diseases), including:

- Respiratory disease
- Cardiovascular disease
- Different cancers
- Erectile dysfunction
- Pregnancy complications
- Dementia and Alzheimer’s disease

Researchers believe the connection is related to the systemic inflammation gum disease causes, as well as the general increase in unfriendly bacteria.

## Turning gingivitis around

The good news is that gingivitis is treatable, meaning you can reverse the condition. At Proud to Smile Dentistry, Dr. Swaintek-Lamb creates a personalized gingivitis treatment plan designed to meet your unique needs. She may suggest that you:

### 1. Floss and brush regularly

One of the top ways to keep your gums healthy and reverse gingivitis is maintaining a consistent oral hygiene routine. This means brushing for at least two minutes, two times a day. Don’t forget to brush along the gum line and use a toothpaste that contains fluoride.

A healthy oral hygiene routine also means [flossing every day](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6478368/). This important step removes food and plaque from between your teeth, areas brushing alone can’t reach. You can also use an antiseptic mouthwash to help eliminate bad bacteria and improve your breath.

### 2. Focus on balanced nutrition and hydration

If you want to reverse gum disease, it’s important to focus on balanced nutrition and staying well hydrated. Stay away from foods that encourage the growth of unhealthy bacteria, like simple starches and sugary treats.

You’ll want to eat a diet centered on whole foods, mostly vegetables, fruits, and lean proteins, like legumes, tofu, and eggs. These foods are high in [antioxidants, vitamin C, and calcium](https://www.urmc.rochester.edu/encyclopedia/content.aspx?c&c), which strengthen your teeth and gums.

Drinking plenty of water so you stay hydrated is also essential in reversing gingivitis. This is because saliva is your mouth’s first [line of defense](https://www.webmd.com/oral-health/dental-health-dry-mouth) against plaque buildup.

### 3. Pay attention to what you put in your mouth

Smoking, vaping, and chewing tobacco can prevent the improvement of gum disease because they [weaken your immune system](https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/campaign/tips/diseases/periodontal-gum-disease.html). This makes it easier for infection to take hold and slows your natural healing process.

Medications that cause dry mouth can also make reversing gum disease more challenging. This is because dry mouth is a sign of [reduced saliva production](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/conditionsandtreatments/Teeth-and-medication), and saliva is a key part of cleaning your mouth and protecting your teeth against plaque and tartar.

### 4. Keep up with regular dental cleanings

Reversing gingivitis involves keeping your gums healthy. Preventive dental care with routine [dental cleanings and exams](/service/cleaning/) are essential for doing just that, as your provider removes plaque and tartar buildup and examines the health of your mouth. To help reverse your gingivitis — and keep it away — schedule appointments with our team every six months.
