Seeing blood in the sink after brushing can be unsettling. It’s easy to ignore, especially if it doesn’t hurt — but bleeding gums are often an early warning sign that you shouldn’t overlook.

At [Proud to Smile Dentistry](/about/) in Bernardsville, New Jersey, [Patricia Swaintek-Lamb, DMD](/doctors/patricia-swaintek-lamb-dmd/), and [our team](/doctors/) help patients identify gum disease early and treat it with [periodontal therapy](/service/periodontal-therapy/) before it becomes more serious. Here’s what you should know.

## What bleeding gums really mean

Bleeding gums usually point to inflammation caused by plaque buildup along the gumline. This early stage of gum disease, called gingivitis, is common and fully treatable.

You might notice redness, swelling, or bleeding when brushing and flossing. Bad breath can also linger. At this stage, the condition is reversible with good oral hygiene and professional care — but it won’t go away on its own.

## When gingivitis becomes more serious

If gingivitis is left untreated, it can progress to periodontitis. This more advanced form of gum disease affects the bone and tissues that support your teeth.

As the condition worsens, your gums may begin to pull away from your teeth, creating pockets where bacteria collect. Teeth may start to feel loose, and chewing may become uncomfortable. Unlike gingivitis, periodontitis requires more involved treatment to control.

Advanced gum disease isn’t reversible — it must be managed long-term, instead.

## When to see a dentist about bleeding gums

Many people wait until they feel pain, but gum disease doesn’t always cause discomfort right away. That’s why it’s important to pay attention to the early signs of gum disease.

You should schedule a visit with our team if you notice:

- Bleeding that happens regularly
- Swollen, tender, or receding gums
- Persistent bad breath ([halitosis](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/conditions-and-diseases/halitosis-bad-breath))
- Changes in the fit of your bite

Catching the problem early can prevent long-term damage and make treatment much simpler.

## What periodontal therapy involves

[Periodontal therapy](/service/periodontal-therapy/) focuses on removing bacteria and helping your gums heal. The type of treatment you need depends on the severity of your condition.

In the early stages, a deep cleaning called [scaling and root planing](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23983-tooth-scaling-and-root-planing) can remove plaque and tartar below the gumline. This helps the gums reattach to the teeth. In more advanced cases, additional treatments or ongoing maintenance may be needed to keep the disease under control.

## How to protect your gums at home

Good daily habits make a big difference. Brushing twice a day and flossing at least once daily helps remove plaque before it can irritate your gums.

It also helps to use a soft-bristled toothbrush and follow your dentist’s recommendations for mouthwash or other products. Having regular [dental cleanings](/service/cleaning/) is just as important, since they remove plaque and tartar buildup you can’t reach or get rid of at home.

## Don’t ignore the signs of gum disease

Bleeding gums may seem like a minor dental concern, but it’s your body’s way of signaling the start of a deeper potential issue. The sooner you address it, the easier it is to treat.
